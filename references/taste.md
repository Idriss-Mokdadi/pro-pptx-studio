# The user's taste (read before every deck or banner)

Built from two rating boards (31 style directions, then their 24 Canva templates, slide types, theme fonts and image sources) and from feedback on test decks. "Same feel" means these traits.

## The rule that matters most
Decks must look like the user's Canva files: **designer art, not generated art**. Build on real templates from the library (`references/template-mode.md`). The user rejected, with strong words, the decks made from code-drawn backgrounds and emoji-style 3D objects ("the lock picture is clearly made by AI", "the background is not good", "why can't you make ones like the pptx files I gave you"). So:
- Backgrounds, photos, 3D renders, decorations: from library templates, the user's own files, or Canva (the user approved Canva-generated images). Never procedural backgrounds or emoji 3D in template mode.
- Generated parts are limited to what looks right when drawn natively: charts, KPI tiles, tables, code windows, timelines, device mockups around real screenshots, icons from Lucide/Phosphor.
- Mixing is welcome: icons from one template, background from another, a timeline from an infographic kit, recolored to the topic. The result must look like one designer made it.

## Fonts follow the topic (chosen on Style Board 2)
- Cyber / hacker: VT323 + Share Tech Mono, Orbitron + Share Tech Mono, Black Ops One + Rajdhani.
- AI / futuristic: Exo 2 (italic display) + Exo 2, Orbitron + Exo 2, Audiowide + Inter.
- Telecom / network: Saira + Inter, Rajdhani + Inter, Sora + Inter, Titillium Web.
- Energy / industrial: Barlow Condensed + Barlow, Teko + Barlow, Big Shoulders Display + Barlow.
- Defense / academic: Bebas Neue + Montserrat, Big Shoulders Display + Inter, Montserrat + Inter.
- Event / hackathon: Bungee, Monoton, Bowlby One or Rubik Mono One + Montserrat/Inter.
A template's own fonts are also fine when they already fit the topic (Pattanakarn on the Cloud Security template). Offer both in the preview.

## Effects they love (use whenever possible)
- **Object in front of the title**: a cut-out object overlapping the big title letters (their favourite: the open book in front of "THESIS DEFENSE"). Use the `depth` kit or a template that already does it.
- **Glow behind a product photo** (the watch on the orange pitch deck): soft colored glow behind a rounded photo or device.
- Hexagon badges, pill labels, HUD frames, glass panels, Morph transitions, step-by-step reveals.

## Slide types they approved
Glass, dark aurora, abstract 3D, cyber neon, HUD, duotone photos, isometric, device mockups, about-me, KPI dashboards, roadmaps, code windows, bento (Ivan Volti: icons and fonts).

## What they rejected
- Code-drawn backgrounds, emoji-style or obviously AI-made 3D objects, generic fonts on a themed talk.
- Plain white minimal / Swiss layouts, consulting-style white slides, light aurora pastels, editorial magazine looks for technical content, neo-brutalism, blueprint paper, thin-grid SaaS dark.
- "Vibe-coded" signs: a giant circle on the cover, numbered lists as the only design, accent bars under titles, alternating white and dark slides, generic blue, lorem-like filler.

## Defaults when the user says nothing
- Template mode first. Show the cover preview (templates x theme fonts) and let them choose; never pick silently when they are present.
- Motion: Morph between slides plus step reveals (`auto`); `click` when they want to reveal each point.
- Language follows the content (French for ENET'Com juries is common; English for IEEE events).
- About-me or team slide only when asked or when the deck is a pitch/competition.

## Feedback from the internship defense deck (October 2026)
The rules below are the user's corrections; SKILL.md lists them as house rules.
- Text never over the central picture of a background; titles always at the top, simple topic names ("Architecture", "Cost Analysis").
- Bullets or short phrases, never paragraphs; no jargon labels ("Terraform", not "HCL"); word groups such as "Sprint 1" on one line.
- Text centered inside its container (pills, hexagons, tiles); closing "Questions" line large.
- Calm backgrounds on text-heavy slides; busy art only on cover, section and closing.
- Loved: numbered hexagon plan cards, the real Azure deployment redrawn as clean tiles with official icons, gradient charts with a glow, logos on a light chip.
- Rejected: charts that only repeat what a screenshot proves, competitor cost comparisons nobody asked for, titles at the bottom of a slide.
